## About the project

**Mr. Brain: the mascot who goes along with the student’s journey on Futura.** Mr. Brain is Editora Futura’s mascot and works as the student’s companion inside the digital platform. His job is to make learning feel closer, warmer and more motivating, staying with the student through activities and stages of development.

He is not just decoration: his presence helps connect the student to the learning experience.

## Challenges

- Give the character shape, from sketches to the final poses in Illustrator.
- Turn the roles set out in the pitch into rules for voice, behavior and animation.
- Animate the illustrations with AI, from a library of 14 states.
- Design an AI conversation that reduces the fear of speaking English.

## Business context

### What is Mr. Brain’s role?

According to the platform pitch, Mr. Brain goes along with the student in five moments:

- **First access:** welcomes the student and helps introduce the journey.
- **Placement:** turns the initial assessment into a mission or challenge, so the student doesn’t feel they are taking a test.
- **Teacher messages:** appears as part of the communication and guidance sent to the student.
- **Challenges:** stays with the student through the experiences and activities.
- **Feedback:** shares positive messages and encourages the student to keep learning.

He is also the conversation partner in the AI role-play and the teacher’s assistant, with class reports and suggested next steps. And he changes with the school stage: in the early years he is a small floating robot that projects holograms to explain words; from middle school to high school, an assistant that “scans” sentences and shows grammar tables.

### Mr. Brain in placement

This is one of the moments where the character matters most. The pitch proposes presenting placement as a quest, a challenge or a starting point, not a traditional test. The goal is to keep the student from feeling pressured or frustrated.

At the end, for example, Mr. Brain can hand over a medal and show a message like:

> “Congratulations! You are now a student of 6th grade and A1!”

Feedback for the student should be positive, without a report of right and wrong answers. Teachers and the publisher, on the other hand, get detailed information about levels and the difficulties identified.

The role-play is Speaking practice with AI, and every conversation has a processing cost. That is why AI cost per conversation and a daily conversation limit per student are among the product questions to validate with the team.

## Hypothesis

**Tested:** a personalized feedback message from Mr. Brain, shown after an activity, increases the likelihood that the student starts the next one.

**To validate in the role-play:** showing up to three clear missions before and during the conversation, offering help inside the chat itself and leaving corrections for the end reduces fear of speaking and abandonment of the simulation.

## Research

**Market references for the role-play:**

- **Duolingo Max (Roleplay):** scenarios written by experts and matched to the student’s point in the course; the character opens the conversation and the activity earns XP.
- **Speak:** three tasks per scenario; the simulation ends when the tasks are done or the conversation reaches a natural end. Independent reviews point to its final feedback as shallow.
- **Babbel Speak:** the student reviews the tasks before starting, can replay or slow down the AI’s speech and ask for a hint; when the student goes off topic, the AI brings them back to the scenario.

**Audit of the authoring flow in the admin panel**, before the redesign: “Save” buttons scattered per section, informational notices in yellow that looked like errors, a disabled “Next” with no explanation, a stepper that didn’t mark completed steps, a “Who am I?” field that didn’t make clear it was the AI persona, duration and attempt limits starting at 0 and no indication of the 3-objective minimum.

## Creation process and illustration

The Mr. Brain illustrations were made in Adobe Illustrator by the project’s designer. In vector, the same drawing scales from an avatar icon to a full-page illustration without losing quality.

From the pitch and the character dossier to the final poses, the work went through five stages:

1. **Sketches:** icon studies in nine panels, with framings, expressions, a silhouette and versions with the Editora Futura brand.
2. **Vector in Illustrator:** the character’s shape, color and details finished in vector.
3. **Poses:** celebrating, dancing, flying, resting, reading, writing, listening, speaking and working with a board, tablet, laptop and phone.
4. **Sequences:** sketches in panels for animated states, such as asleep, waking up and excited.
5. **AI animation:** each of the 14 states got a standard prompt to animate the illustration, and the video team produced the clips.

### Character fundamentals

- **Shape:** a transparent dome with the brain on show, round glasses, headphones and antennas, and a white body with purple details.
- **Silhouette:** the silhouette test shows whether the character is recognizable by shape alone, even at small sizes. It shows up in the very first icon studies.
- **Expression:** five base emotions (joy, pride, curiosity, support and calm) and gestures that read even without audio.
- **Color:** purple, violet, pink and lavender on white.
- **Rules:** Mr. Brain never fails the student, never rushes and never corrects mid-conversation.

## Decisions and trade-offs

### A key UX decision: the student is the one who achieves

Mr. Brain acts as a mentor, guide and cheerleader, not as the hero of the achievements. For example:

- The student earns a medal; Mr. Brain hands it over and celebrates with them.
- The student moves up the leaderboard; Mr. Brain can congratulate them, but never appears as the one in that position.
- The student finishes an activity; the character recognizes the effort without taking the result for himself.

This distinction makes gamification value the student’s authorship and agency.

### Other decisions

- **The character never disapproves.** Mr. Brain doesn’t express frustration or use negative correction, irony or a rushed tone. The guide’s principle: he doesn’t grade, correct or judge; he accompanies, encourages and celebrates.
- **Corrections at the end, not during the conversation.** Correcting every turn would break the rhythm. Corrections go to the final feedback, in “You said / More natural” cards, using a warning color rather than error red, so they don’t feel like punishment.
- **Going off topic without an error message.** The character brings the student back to the scenario within the conversation itself.
- **Speak or type.** The student can finish the conversation only speaking, only typing or switching between them. The transcript is always visible: audio is never the only way to receive the character’s lines.
- **XP doesn’t depend on the AI.** If feedback is slow or fails, XP and missions still appear.
- **Animate only when it helps.** The usage matrix avoids repeating animations in every activity: if it doesn’t add clarity or reassurance, it’s out.
- **Save per step in the admin panel.** A single footer per step (Back, Save draft and Next step) instead of the many per-section “Save” buttons. It depends on the API accepting the whole step; it was logged as a point to confirm with Engineering. Details in the [Editora Futura case study](/en/projects/editora-futura#admin-panel-one-footer-per-step).
- **Left to validate:** turn or time limits per scenario, AI cost per conversation, the character’s synthesized voice and accent, and the language of the feedback explanations.

## Solution

### How the character is used in the experience

Based on what the pitch proposes, Mr. Brain’s part in the experience falls into four uses:

1. **Welcome and guidance:** greets the student, presents the steps and helps explain what they need to do.
2. **Challenges and discovery:** introduces missions, gives activities context and encourages the student to keep going.
3. **Achievement and recognition:** celebrates progress, presents medals and recognizes the student’s effort.
4. **Encouragement and feedback:** encourages another try and shares warm messages while the student learns.

![Board of Mr. Brain’s four uses, each with a pose: welcome and guidance, challenges and discovery, achievement and recognition, encouragement and feedback](img:mb_usos_en)

### From the pitch to a behavior guide

The pitch defines these general roles, but it doesn’t include a full manual of personality, lines, expressions or usage rules for the character. Part of it was documented in the project: the voice in the interface, with copy per module, and the animation guide, with emotions, gestures and states. These pieces can be brought together into a single behavior and usage guide for Mr. Brain.

### Voice and tone

Mr. Brain’s base persona is smart, friendly and motivating; the tone is positive, clear and non-judgmental; the role is to guide, engage, give feedback and set up challenges. He never exposes mistakes negatively or compares students. Each module has a communication goal (examples translated from Portuguese):

- **Speaking, to reduce embarrassment and fear:** “This is a safe space. Speak without fear.” and “It doesn’t need to be perfect, it needs to be tried.”
- **Retry, without frustration:** “Almost there! How about trying again?”
- **Check your Proficiency, assessment without pressure:** “No grade. Just growth.”
- **Teacher, pedagogical support:** “I noticed some students are struggling with Listening. Want to see the details?”
- **Kids version, short sentences:** “Shall we play English?”

### Behavior and animation

The guide sets a medium-to-slow speech pace for the youngest and medium for older students, five base emotions (joy, pride, curiosity, support and calm), gestures that read even without sound and 14 reusable animation states, such as welcome, encouragement during an activity, new attempt, speaking (listening and approval) and waiting. A second library covers micro-states that reduce anxiety, such as progress saved and activity paused.

To produce at scale, each state has a standardized prompt to animate the illustration with AI in 2–5 second clips that work without sound, plus a file naming convention for the video team.

The achievement and study poses go along with the moments when the student earns a medal, listens to audio or investigates a new word.

### Role-play: authoring in the publisher’s admin panel

Authoring became a 4-step flow: introduction, student instructions, AI settings and objectives. “Who am I?” became “AI persona”, with a helper line; duration and attempts got units (min, times); objectives have a progress counter (“2 of 3 minimum objectives”); the stepper marks completed steps; and the simulation preview stays fixed on the side while the AI is configured.

### Role-play: the student conversation

- **Before:** on a single screen, the student sees the scenario, their role, the character they will talk to, up to three missions, vocabulary chips with audio and three rules (“Speak or type in English”, “Stuck? Use a Hint”, “There are no wrong answers: feedback comes at the end”). An optional microphone test prevents audio errors in the middle of the simulation.
- **During:** a focus-mode chat with a missions panel, replay, 0.75x speed, translate and hint. Every wait has an indicator (“Emma is typing…”, and after 10 seconds “This is taking longer than usual”, with “Try again”) and every error has a way out (unrecognized audio, no connection, AI service failure). The student can report an AI message.
- **After:** a short celebration (XP, missions and time), what went well, up to three corrections with a one-line explanation and “Practice this sentence”, words to save for review and the full conversation.

### Conversation quality: fine-tuning

On the fine-tuning screen, the team validates, rejects or edits AI conversation turns. The redesign moved the main actions to the top (“Run fine-tuning” and “Run history”), replaced four unlabeled icons with inline actions and made the status readable.

## Validation

Mr. Brain’s personalized feedback message raised the rate of starting the next activity from 28.1% to 32.4% (+4.3 pp, p = 0.0008) in an A/B test with 12,432 students, run in 2025. The full numbers are in the [Editora Futura case study](/en/projects/editora-futura#validation).

The student role-play flows have defined acceptance criteria, for example:

- At 1280 × 720, the student sees scenario, role and missions without scrolling.
- The student can finish the conversation only speaking, only typing or switching between both.
- Every wait has an indicator and every error has a way out.
- XP and missions appear within 1 second, even when feedback is slow.

## How it was measured

- **Goal and hypothesis:** for feedback, get students to move on to the next activity (hypothesis tested in an A/B test). For the role-play, reduce fear of speaking and abandonment of the simulation with up to three clear missions, help inside the chat and corrections at the end (hypothesis still to validate).
- **North star metric:** rate of starting the next activity, for Mr. Brain’s personalized feedback.
- **Supporting metrics:** activity completion, average time on activity and return within 7 days, plus the assistant’s reach, which got to 15,000 users.
- **Guardrail:** for the role-play, AI cost per conversation and a daily conversation limit per student are among the questions to validate with the team.
- **Collection:** a 2025 A/B test with 12,432 students, in the Futura Analytics experiments panel. The baseline is the control group, and the details are in the [Editora Futura case study](/en/projects/editora-futura#how-it-was-measured).

## Strategic view

Mr. Brain works as an element of identity and continuity on the platform. While activities, challenges and content change, he stays as a familiar presence that helps the student understand where they are, what they need to do and what they have achieved.

## Learnings

- A character needs rules before it needs lines. Defining what Mr. Brain never does (disapprove, rush, correct mid-conversation) kept copy, animation and prompts speaking with the same voice.

## In short

Mr. Brain is the companion on the learning journey. He guides, encourages, celebrates and brings the student closer to the platform, but the one who learns, grows and achieves is always the student.
