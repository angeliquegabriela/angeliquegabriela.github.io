## About the project

**Editora Futura** is an English learning platform. It extends the textbook experience, starting with students using the Happy English series, and connects students, teachers, coordinators, families and the publisher in a single digital experience.

The platform covers the four language skills (Listening, Speaking, Reading and Writing) with adaptive placement, learning paths per school year, interactive activities, artificial intelligence, gamification and performance tracking.

## Challenges

- Connect the printed book to the platform, from the book code to first access, placement and learning paths.
- Create gamification and a single interaction pattern for the four skills.
- Simplify AI role-play authoring in the admin panel.
- Give Mr. Brain, the platform’s mascot, a consistent look and voice in the interface.
- Validate with an A/B test whether Mr. Brain’s feedback leads students to the next activity.

## Business context

Editora Futura publishes English textbooks for schools and school networks. The platform extends the book: a code printed in it unlocks access and starts a continuous journey of placement, activities, feedback, progression and follow-up. The goal is to turn English into a continuous, personalized, interactive and measurable journey, in which students learn, practice, get feedback and follow their own progress.

The previous digital experience was focused on Speaking. Evolving it was not about adding activities: it meant rethinking how content is accessed, how students progress, how teachers follow their classes and how data supports teaching decisions.

### How the platform works

The student experience is organized as a five-step journey:

1. **First access and sign-up:** the student uses the book code, creates a password and fills in personal and school details.
2. **Placement:** an adaptive assessment identifies their English level by the CEFR, with a proposed integration with the BNCC, Brazil’s national curriculum base.
3. **Learning paths:** the student follows the path for their school year, with units and activities across the four skills.
4. **Activities and challenges:** interactive exercises, videos, quizzes, AI activities, role-plays and challenges.
5. **Assessment and progress:** the student follows their progress, gets feedback and earns points and badges.

## UX process

The work was organized in a Double Diamond. Design Thinking shaped how each stage was run, Jobs to Be Done framed the needs of each user type, and Lean UX turned bets into testable hypotheses.

- **Discover:** a diagnosis of the digital experience, which was focused on Speaking, and an audit of the role-play authoring flow in the admin panel. It produced six opportunities, from limited learning to limited formative assessment.
- **Define:** the product bet, moving from an exercise list to a platform guided by journeys, and the jobs of each user type. Students want to know what to do next and see their own progress; teachers want to follow and guide the class; the publisher wants data to support pedagogical decisions.
- **Develop:** a six-moment journey (access, placement, learning, feedback and progression, interaction and follow-up), one interaction pattern for the four skills, gamification and the admin panel redesign, all in the Futura Design System.
- **Deliver and learn:** in the Lean UX loop, the personalized feedback hypothesis became an A/B test with a control group, primary metric and stopping criterion defined before the result.

## Hypothesis

The product bet was to move from a list of exercises to a platform guided by journeys, progression and follow-up, with an assistant that gives the experience continuity.

The hypothesis tested in an experiment was specific: **Mr. Brain’s personalized feedback, shown after an activity, increases the likelihood that the student starts the next one.**

## Research

The diagnosis of the existing experience pointed to six opportunities:

- **Limited learning:** integrate the four skills and vary activity formats.
- **Little teacher autonomy:** give teachers tools to follow and guide their classes.
- **Progress hard to see:** make each student’s progress clear and trackable.
- **Little interaction:** take practice beyond individual activities.
- **Distant families:** bring learning closer to the family context.
- **Limited formative assessment:** connect activities, feedback and indicators along the journey.

The diagnosis also showed that the previous experience depended entirely on an internet connection, which led to the proposal of online and offline activities.

### Admin panel audit

Before the redesign, the role-play authoring flow in the admin panel went through an audit. The findings, organized by Nielsen’s heuristics, became the changes in the new flow: a stepper that marks completed steps, “AI persona” instead of “Who am I?”, one footer per step, units on the duration and attempt fields and a counter for the minimum objectives.

## Decisions and trade-offs

- **Value before sign-up.** First access links the student to their school, class and teacher and ends with a short interest questionnaire (series, games or music). Nothing is asked before the student sees the platform’s value.
- **Placement that doesn’t feel like a test.** Two 5–7 minute steps, the second adapted to performance in the first. Mr. Brain frames it as a starting point, and the result shows school year + CEFR level, with the proposed alignment with Brazil’s national curriculum (BNCC).
- **One language for four skills.** Listening, Speaking, Reading and Writing have their own interactions, but navigation, progress indicators and activity structure follow the same pattern, so they don’t become four different products.
- **Gamification without constant competition.** XP, badges, levels, streaks and unlocks give progress context; Check your Proficiency is not graded.

### Admin panel: one footer per step

- **Decision:** the many per-section “Save” buttons became a single footer per step, with Back, Save draft and Next step.
- **Trade-off:** this depends on the API accepting the whole step in one save. If it saves each block separately, the back end has to change or the per-section buttons come back. It was logged as a point to confirm with Engineering.
- **What was left out:** confirmation when removing a role-play objective. The proposal is a quick notice with an undo option, instead of an “Are you sure?” modal.

## Solution

The journey connects six moments without losing focus on the student: access, placement, learning, feedback and progression, interaction and follow-up.

- **A path per school year:** 8 units, 12 activities per unit, 4 Leveling Up challenges per unit and a checkpoint (Check your Proficiency) every 2 units, with interactive exercises, videos, quizzes, AI activities, role-plays and challenges.
- **Practice beyond the individual:** forum, events, Speaking Club and live activities, with participation and safety rules designed for schools.
- **Short games:** Mr. Brain on the cloud practices vocabulary in 60 seconds, with answers by text or voice and a class word cloud as the result.
- **Mr. Brain across the journey:** Editora Futura’s mascot appears in placement, messages, challenges, guidance and feedback, as a guide, conversation partner in the AI role-play and teacher assistant with class reports. He doesn’t take the student’s place at the center: he guides, encourages and celebrates. The voice and behavior design is in the [Mr. Brain case study](/en/projects/mr-brain).
- **Admin panel:** role-play authoring in 4 steps (introduction, student instructions, AI settings and objectives), with a simulation preview. Everything follows the Futura Design System, with 44 color variables and light and dark modes.

### Product pillars

- **Personalized learning:** adaptive placement and activities suited to each student’s development.
- **Gamification:** XP, badges, challenges and achievements to drive engagement.
- **Artificial intelligence:** support for speaking, pronunciation and role-play.
- **Teacher autonomy:** teachers unlock units and follow progress.
- **Social interaction:** forums, collaborative activities and communication between students and teachers.
- **Follow-up:** individual and class reports for teachers, coordinators and families.
- **Flexibility:** online and offline activities.

### Badges, lives, goals, leaderboard and ranks

The platform’s gamification combines five mechanics:

- **Badges:** recognize achievements along the journey and are shown on the student home.
- **Lives:** the student loses a life with each mistake, and lives refill over time.
- **Goals:** daily practice goals give the student a clear objective for each day.
- **Class leaderboard:** the student follows their own position in the class. Mr. Brain congratulates whoever moves up, but never appears in the spot himself.
- **Ranks:** the student moves up in rank by earning XP, which comes from activities, challenges and role-plays.

![Gamification board with five mechanics: badges, lives, goals, class leaderboard and ranks](img:ef_game_en)

The gamification icons share one style across the platform, from the reward chest to medals, Level Up and XP.

### Engagement dynamics

Gamification combines five dynamics: progression, achievement, challenge, social practice and learning without pressure. In all of them, the student is the one who achieves: Mr. Brain hands over the medal and celebrates, but never takes the result for himself.

![Board of the platform’s engagement dynamics: progression, achievement, challenge, social practice and no pressure](img:ef_dyn_en)

### UX writing: Mr. Brain’s voice

Mr. Brain’s voice in the interface follows a smart, friendly and motivating base persona, with a positive, clear and non-judgmental tone. He never exposes mistakes negatively or compares students, and each module has a communication goal, from fear-free Speaking to assessment without pressure.

![UX writing board with Mr. Brain’s persona and tone, what he never does, copy examples per module and the role-play microcopy](img:ef_writing_en)

### Conversational AI: Speaking role-play

The role-play is Speaking practice with AI. The flow runs from authoring in the publisher’s panel to the student’s conversation. Before it, the student sees the scenario, missions and vocabulary; during it, they can speak, type or switch, with tools to replay, slow the speech down and ask for a hint; after it, they see what went well and up to three corrections. On the fine-tuning screen, the team validates, rejects or edits AI turns.

![Conversational AI board with the role-play flow: authoring in the panel, before, during and after the conversation, and the quality of the AI’s answers](img:ef_ia_en)

## Validation

Personalized feedback raised the rate of starting the next activity from 28.1% in the control group to 32.4% in the treatment group: an absolute difference of 4.3 percentage points and a relative improvement of 15.4%, with p = 0.0008.

| Metric | Control | Treatment | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starting the next activity (primary) | 28.1% | 32.4% | +4.3 pp |
| Activity completion | 64.7% | 66.2% | +1.5 pp |
| Average time on activity | 05:12 | 05:33 | +21 s |
| Return within 7 days | 38.2% | 41.0% | +2.8 pp |

> Reading taken at 67% of the minimum sample; only the primary metric supports a causal effect.

The 95% confidence interval for the primary metric runs from 2.1 to 6.6 pp. Among secondary metrics, time on activity (p = 0.0013) and return within 7 days (p = 0.0031) also showed significant differences; activity completion did not (p = 0.1592).

## How it was measured

- **Goal and hypothesis:** get students to move on to the next activity. The hypothesis: Mr. Brain’s personalized feedback, shown after an activity, increases the likelihood that the student starts the next one.
- **North star metric:** rate of starting the next activity. It is the behavior the feedback aims to trigger, measured right after it.
- **Supporting metrics:** activity completion, average time on activity and return within 7 days, to see whether the effect goes beyond the next click.
- **Guardrail:** the experiment panel does not define a formal guardrail.
- **Guardrail, detail:** [PREENCHER: did any metric make sure the feedback did no harm, such as load time or complaints?]
- **Collection:** Futura Analytics experiments panel, in 2025. An A/B test with a control group and 12,432 students (6,214 in control and 6,218 in treatment): all active students aged 8 to 17, on web, iOS and Android, 50% in each group. The baseline is the control group. Planned stopping criterion: 95% confidence on the primary metric and the minimum sample reached. The numbers below come from a reading taken before that point, at 67% of the minimum sample.
- **Result (before → after):** from 28.1% to 32.4% in starting the next activity (+4.3 pp, 95% confidence interval from 2.1 to 6.6 pp, p = 0.0008). Time on activity and return within 7 days also rose significantly; completion did not.
- **What changed in the decision:** [PREENCHER: after the test, was the personalized feedback rolled out to all students? Did anything change in Mr. Brain or the roadmap?]
- **Data limitations:** the reading was taken while the experiment was still running, with 67% of the planned minimum sample. The experiment supports a causal effect only on the primary metric: completion, time and return need their own tests.

## Post-launch results

- Mr. Brain, the platform’s AI assistant, reached 15,000 users.
- Book, digital access, placement, learning paths, four skills and progress now work as a single journey, and the foundation was ready to extend the platform to the early years with [Futura Kids](/en/projects/futura-kids).

## Learnings

- The secondary metrics also improved, but the experiment only supports a causal effect on the primary metric. Completion, time and return need their own tests.
- Next time: record the minimum detectable effect before the test and share the result only after the stopping criterion, with the full minimum sample.
