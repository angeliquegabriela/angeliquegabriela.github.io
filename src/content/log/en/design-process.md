The design process is an essential attribute of well maintained DesignOps. It is a significant part of the job to understand how to work with different tasks and successfully contribute to the actual product and not to the design concepts.

## The problem

When we faced the different frameworks and methodologies for design work in our company, we realised that it didn't help us achieve final results efficiently. Various product teams used to work according to different frameworks, sometimes even on their own.

> Different frameworks and methodologies for design work in our company.

## How we fixed it

In the beginning, I decided to gather all the information about frameworks to fix the problem. After analysing it, I finished with several frameworks:

- Design Thinking
- Double Diamond
- Hook Model
- H.E.A.R.T.

Each of these frameworks has its pros and cons. The main challenge was to structure it and use only those points that helped simplify our company's problem-solving process.

After that, I wrote our Design process allocated to our product teams and our unique working frameworks. The document was reviewed by the team before being published.

As a result, we fixed it in Notion (we use it for our database). Our Design Process consists of the following parts:

![The Design Process documented in Notion.](img:g13)

### 1. How to get and accept a new task

- The task and metrics
- The different scale of tasks
- User Story Mapping
- DoD and DoR

### 2. How to work with a task

- Hypothesis
- CJM
- Research
- Work with data
- Variety of decisions
- Working with texts
- Usability tests
- Final solution

### 3. Developing and review

- [Design Check](/en/log/design-check) (another process I described here)
- Figma files
- Designer to Developer
- [Front Review](/en/log/front-review) (the process described here)

### 4. Production

- Production check
- Users feedback
- Changes implementations

## Results & learnings

Forming this process helped us standardise the process of work not only for product designers but also for developers, product owners and product teams in general.

It simplified the onboarding process for new designers and helped others structure their knowledge regarding the work with digital products.
